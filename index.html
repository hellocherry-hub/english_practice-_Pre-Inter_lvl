A. Sentence transformation

Rewrite each sentence so it means the same as the first one. Use the word in capitals. Do not change the word. Use 2–6 words, including the word in capitals.

B. Gap-fill story 1: Hiro's new job

Choose the correct word from the box for each gap. Write one word or phrase for each gap.

got · boss · were · deal · usually · goes · once in a while · is going to · should · never

Hiro (1) ___ a job at a small publishing company last year. His (2) ___ was friendly, and his colleagues (3) ___ very helpful. Hiro has to (4) ___ with many customers, so he has learned to be patient. He (5) ___ works late on Mondays, but he is hardly ever tired on Tuesdays. In the evenings he (6) ___ to the gym, but (7) ___ he stays home to read. Next month he (8) ___ take a trip to Vietnam with his sister.

C. Gap-fill story 2: Lina's big decision

Choose the correct word or phrase from the box for each gap. Write one word or phrase for each gap.

made · decided · wanted · never · had to · must · mustn't · going to · gave · should

Last year Lina (1) ___ a big decision. She (2) ___ to quit her job and open a café. Her parents thought it was risky, but she (3) ___ to try. "I've (4) ___ been so excited," she says. Before she opened, she (5) ___ learn about food safety. "You (6) ___ study hard," she laughs, "and you (7) ___ forget the rules!" Now she is (8) ___ open a second café next summer.

D. Error correction

E. Crossword-style clues

Write one word for each clue. The number in brackets is the number of letters.

F. Odd one out

In each row, three words belong together. Write the odd word and say why in a few words.

G. Questions about you

Answer in full sentences. Aim for 2–4 sentences each.

H. Postcard

Write a postcard of 50–70 words to a friend.

Imagine you are on holiday in a city you like. In your postcard:
• say where you are and what you did yesterday (past simple)
• say one thing you have never done (never + present perfect)
• say one plan for tomorrow (going to or the present continuous)
• invite your friend to visit (Would you like to…?)
• use at least two linking words (and, but, so, because)
